# Open-data coverage assessment — English from Hungarian

Checked: 2026-11-05

## Verdict

**The available public data is sufficient to build a useful, course-aligned Hungarian-language companion for the English-from-Hungarian Duolingo course.** It is much stronger than a generic CEFR map because the public course page exposes a 1005-unit sequence and explicitly names several grammar objectives in its Intro, A1 and A2 modules.

The data is not a live official Duolingo API. The exact content in the learner's app can differ due to account-specific rollout or later course revisions. B1 and B2 units are mainly scenario/communication-goal based, so detailed grammar links at those levels need to be treated as pedagogical candidates.

## Source B — English Grammar Profile

https://englishprofile.org/?menu=english-grammar-profile

The English Grammar Profile is a free online reference that maps more than 1200 English grammatical structures to CEFR levels, based on learner-corpus analysis.

It organizes content such as:
- articles, determiners and quantifiers;
- present, past and future forms;
- modality;
- negation and questions;
- clauses, conditionals and relative clauses;
- passives and reported speech;
- verb patterns, phrasal verbs and prepositions;
- pronouns, adverbs, adjectives and discourse markers.

### Value

This is the strongest structural reference for deciding which grammar explanations are appropriate at each level. It helps prevent the tutor from treating B2 grammar as a flat list or teaching it before the learner is ready.

### Limitation

The English Grammar Profile is a CEFR reference, not the Duolingo course syllabus. A grammar point's CEFR label does not prove that it appears in a specific Duolingo unit.

## Source D — Council of Europe CEFR

https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions

The CEFR describes communicative ability and grammatical accuracy at each level. At A1/A2 it emphasizes a limited repertoire of simple structures and predictable communication. B1 emphasizes reasonably accurate familiar routines and patterns. B2 expects good control of simple and some complex forms.

### Value

Use the CEFR to judge what a learner should be able to do and how precise an explanation should be.

### Limitation

The CEFR is not a detailed English grammar syllabus and does not define Duolingo unit objectives.

## Source E — Hungarian/English contrastive reference

### English articles
Kovács, Éva (2023), *On the Use of Articles in English and in Hungarian*:
https://real.mtak.hu/229141/

The paper discusses definite, indefinite and zero articles, including generic reference and proper nouns. It directly identifies article use as a difficulty for Hungarian learners.

### Word order
Aradi, András (1997), *Word Order and Sentence Articulation: Hungarian-English Contrastive Aspects*:
https://pp.bme.hu/so/article/view/6504

The paper contrasts English subject-prominent structure with Hungarian word order organized around communicative information structure.

### Present perfect
Nagy, Tünde, *Teaching the Present Perfect to Native Hungarian Learners* (in the 2019 multilingualism volume):
https://real.mtak.hu/104006/1/Pletl-Kovacs_Soknyelvuseg%20es%20tobbnyelvuseg.pdf

The paper discusses the mismatch between Hungarian past-tense forms and English tense/aspect distinctions, especially the continuative use of the present perfect.

### High-priority contrastive teaching points

1. **To be:** English requires a copula in ordinary present-tense nominal/adjectival predicates where Hungarian can omit it in third person.
2. **Do-support:** questions and negation in present/past simple require `do/does/did`, except with `be` and modal auxiliaries.
3. **Articles:** English definite, indefinite and zero article choices do not map one-to-one to Hungarian.
4. **Word order:** English has a more fixed subject–verb–object base order and generally requires an expressed subject.
5. **Tense/aspect:** present perfect, past continuous and other aspectual contrasts cannot be translated mechanically from Hungarian past tense.
6. **Prepositions:** English prepositions do not correspond one-to-one to Hungarian case suffixes.
7. **Countability:** countable/uncountable distinctions affect articles and quantifiers.
8. **Verb patterns:** gerund/infinitive selection and phrasal verbs need pattern learning, not direct word-for-word translation.

These are priority teaching areas, not a claim that every Hungarian learner makes every listed error.

## Recommended teaching model

1. **Duolingo path layer:** current module, unit, topic and explicit grammar labels.
2. **English grammar layer:** EGP/British Council scope and level-appropriate prerequisites.
3. **Hungarian contrast layer:** explain why literal Hungarian transfer fails in articles, copula, do-support, word order, tense/aspect, prepositions and verb patterns.
4. **Comprehension layer:** one short interactive question at a time; check recognition, meaning, choice and application.
5. **Progress layer:** mark concepts introduced, understood, stable or needing review.

The learner continues to use Duolingo for vocabulary exposure, listening, repeated sentence practice and course progression. ChatGPT explains grammar and language logic; it must not create a duplicate vocabulary course.

## Final assessment

The open data is sufficient to prepare a genuinely course-aligned Hungarian-language companion for the English-from-Hungarian course. The early modules are especially well supported because grammar objectives are explicitly named. B1/B2 can be aligned to communicative themes with EGP/CEFR grammar references, while preserving uncertainty about exact Duolingo unit-level grammar.
